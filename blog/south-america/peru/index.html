Peru is a country of ancient civilizations, extraordinary landscapes and some of the most celebrated food in the world. Spanish is an official language, along with Quechua and Aymara. The Andes hold Peru's most famous treasure, Machu Picchu, reached from Cusco and the Sacred Valley, as well as Lake Titicaca, Colca Canyon and a network of hiking trails through high mountain scenery.

Lima is a world-class culinary capital, where you can try ceviche, causa and other Peruvian classics. Along the coast, the Nazca Lines and desert landscapes add another layer of history, while the Amazon, reached from cities such as Puerto Maldonado or Iquitos, offers rainforest lodges and abundant wildlife.

For the Andes and Machu Picchu, the drier months from May to September are generally the most popular. The coast has its own rhythm and is often misty in the cooler months, while the Amazon is greener and rainier from around November to April.

Peru: Money

Peru's currency is the sol (PEN), written with the symbol S/. US dollars are sometimes accepted in tourist hotels and agencies, but everyday prices are in soles.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards work well in hotels, restaurants and shops in Lima, Cusco and other cities. Markets, small eateries, colectivos and many towns in the Andes and Amazon are cash only.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are widely available in cities and tourist towns. Use machines inside banks or malls when you can. Fees and withdrawal limits vary, so plan ahead before heading to remote areas. Choose to be charged in soles if the machine offers a conversion to your home currency.
  • Tipping: Around 10% is customary in restaurants if a service charge is not already included. A suggested tip may appear on the bill, so check before adding more. Guides and drivers are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Licensed casas de cambio and banks are safer than street changers. Bring clean US dollar notes if you plan to exchange cash, as damaged bills may be refused.
  • Common scams: Counterfeit soles and US dollars do circulate, so learn the look of genuine notes and check bills you receive. Count your change, and keep your card in sight when you pay.

Venezuela is a country of extraordinary natural beauty, from Caribbean islands and the Andes to the vast table-top mountains of the Guiana Highlands. Spanish is the official language. Angel Falls, the world's highest uninterrupted waterfall, plunges from a tepui in Canaima National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The archipelago of Los Roques is known for white-sand cays and clear water, while the Andes around Mérida and the wetlands of the Orinoco Delta show two more of the country's many faces.

Before planning a trip, check your government's current official travel advisories, as conditions and safety recommendations can change.

The dry season, roughly December to April, is generally the most practical time for much of the country. Angel Falls often has a stronger flow during the wetter months, though travel can be more complicated then.

Venezuela: Money

Venezuela's official currency is the bolívar (VES), but US dollars are widely used in everyday transactions. Prices and exchange conditions can change quickly, so check the latest information before you travel, and review your government's current official travel advisories before planning a trip.

  • Cash vs. card: Bring enough clean US dollars in small bills, as change can be hard to get. Many hotels, shops and restaurants in larger cities accept dollars, bolívares or cards, but foreign cards do not always work, so never rely on plastic alone.
  • ATMs and fees: Do not depend on ATMs. Many machines do not accept foreign cards, run out of cash or are out of service. Plan to arrive with the cash you will need.
  • Tipping: Tipping practices vary, and cash in small US dollar bills is the most practical way to leave a little extra for good service.
  • Exchanging money: Exchange currency only at authorized banks or official exchange offices. Informal or street exchanges are illegal and can carry penalties, as well as a risk of counterfeit notes. Rates can move from one day to the next.
  • Common scams: Be extra careful with card payments and inspect notes you receive. Avoid unofficial money changers, and keep cash secure and out of sight.

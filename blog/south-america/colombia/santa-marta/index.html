Founded in 1525, Santa Marta is one of the oldest cities in Colombia and the perfect base for exploring some of the country's most spectacular nature. It sits between the Caribbean Sea and the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, the world's highest coastal mountain range, so beaches and mountains are never far apart.

Tayrona National Park is the star attraction, with jungle trails that open onto palm-fringed beaches and giant boulders. Adventurous travelers can trek several days to Ciudad Perdida (the Lost City), an ancient archaeological site hidden in the mountains. Cool off in Minca, a hillside village of coffee farms, waterfalls and birdwatching, and visit the Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino, where Simón Bolívar spent his final days.

December to April is the dry season and the most popular time to visit. Tayrona closes for a few weeks at certain times of the year to let nature recover, so check the park's schedule before planning your trip.

Travel tips

  • Getting there: Simón Bolívar International Airport (SMR) has domestic flights from Bogotá and other Colombian cities.
  • Getting around: Taxis and ride-hailing work well in the city. Local buses run along the coast toward Tayrona National Park, about an hour away.
  • Food to try: Fresh fried fish with patacones (fried green plantains) and coconut rice.
  • Planning tip: Check Tayrona's opening dates, entry requirements and fees before you go. The Ciudad Perdida trek can only be done with a licensed tour operator.
  • Health tip: Bring strong sunscreen, insect repellent and plenty of water, especially for jungle hikes.

Barranquilla is a warm, welcoming Caribbean city best known for its Carnival, one of the biggest in the world and recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. Here music, dance and color are part of everyday life, and the city's friendly, festive spirit is contagious.

If you visit during Carnival, expect parades, costumes, cumbia and days of celebration. The rest of the year, explore the Museo del Caribe to learn about the region's history and culture, and take an evening walk along the Gran Malecón, a riverside promenade on the Magdalena River with restaurants and views. Fans of Barranquilla-born Shakira can visit her statue, and Bocas de Ceniza, where the Magdalena meets the Caribbean Sea, makes an interesting excursion.

Carnival takes place over the four days before Ash Wednesday, usually in February or early March. December to April is the drier season and generally the best time to visit.

Travel tips

  • Getting there: Ernesto Cortissoz International Airport (BAQ) is located in neighboring Soledad, a short drive from the city.
  • Getting around: Ride-hailing apps and taxis are the easiest ways to get around the city.
  • Food to try: Arepa de huevo, butifarra (a small local sausage), bollo de yuca and arroz de lisa, a traditional rice dish with fish.
  • Carnival tip: If you plan to visit during Carnival, book accommodation well in advance, as prices rise and rooms fill up quickly.
  • Comfort tip: Barranquilla is hot year-round, so wear light clothing, use sunscreen and drink plenty of water.

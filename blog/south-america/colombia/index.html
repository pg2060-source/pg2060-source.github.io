Colombia

Andean cities, colonial towns and two coastlines full of color and rhythm.

Colombia is one of the most diverse countries in the world, with coastlines on both the Caribbean Sea and the Pacific Ocean, the Andes running through its heart and a remarkable variety of wildlife. It's also a country of warm, welcoming people, world-famous coffee, vibrant music and a growing reputation as one of South America's most exciting destinations. Spanish is the official language.

Our Colombia section covers five places with very different personalities. In the Andes, Bogotá offers museums and mountain views, while nearby Villa de Leyva is a perfectly preserved colonial town. On the Caribbean coast, Santa Marta is the gateway to Tayrona National Park, Cartagena enchants with its walled old city, and Barranquilla celebrates one of the world's biggest Carnivals.

Colombia: Money

Colombia's currency is the Colombian peso (COP), shown with a $ sign. Because prices often run into thousands of pesos, take a second look at the zeros before you pay.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants and shops in big cities like Bogotá, Cartagena and Barranquilla. In small towns such as Villa de Leyva, and for street food, markets and local buses, carry cash.
  • ATMs and fees: Use ATMs inside banks, shopping malls or supermarkets, preferably during the day. Many machines charge a withdrawal fee, and the amount you can take out at once varies by bank.
  • Tipping: Many restaurants suggest a voluntary 10% service charge ("propina voluntaria") on the bill. You'll usually be asked whether you'd like to include it, and you can say no.
  • Exchanging money: Licensed exchange offices (casas de cambio) generally offer better rates than airports. Bring your passport, since it's usually required. Avoid changing money with people on the street.
  • Common scams: Check notes for counterfeits, agree on taxi fares before getting in where taxis don't use meters (common in Cartagena), and use ride-hailing apps or official taxi stands when you can.

Villa de Leyva

Colombia

A few hours north of Bogotá in the department of Boyacá, Villa de Leyva feels like stepping back in time. Founded in 1572, this beautifully preserved colonial town is known for its whitewashed buildings, cobblestone streets and its enormous Plaza Mayor, one of the largest town squares in Colombia.

Spend your days wandering the town's quiet lanes, cafés and craft shops, then head out into the surrounding high desert landscape. Visit the El Fósil museum, home to a remarkable fossil of a prehistoric marine reptile (a reminder that this area was once under the sea), explore the whimsical Casa Terracota, a house built entirely of clay, and see the turquoise pools of Pozos Azules. The area is also lovely for hiking, cycling and horseback riding.

The drier months, December to February and July to August, are ideal. Visit in August for the kite festival, or in early December for the Festival of Lights, when fireworks fill the sky over the plaza.

Travel tips

  • Getting there: There's no airport. Most visitors travel by road from Bogotá, about 3 to 4 hours by car or bus (buses usually connect via Tunja).
  • Getting around: The town center is compact and walkable. To reach nearby sights, rent a bicycle, hire a local driver or join a tour.
  • Food to try: Hearty Boyacá dishes, fresh trout and local sweets sold around the main square.
  • Money tip: Bring some cash, as smaller shops and restaurants may not accept cards.
  • Comfort tip: Wear sturdy shoes for the cobblestones, and bring a jacket since nights can be chilly.

Ecuador

Andes, Amazon and the Galápagos, all packed into one compact country.

Ecuador packs the Andes, the Amazon, a Pacific coastline and the Galápagos Islands into a compact country that is easy to explore. Spanish is the official language, and Kichwa and Shuar are also official. High in the Andes, Quito's historic center, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is one of the best preserved in the Americas. Nearby, you can visit the equatorial line and the bustling market town of Otavalo. Further south, Cuenca charms with colonial architecture and a relaxed pace.

The Galápagos Islands are the country's most famous destination, home to giant tortoises, marine iguanas and wildlife that helped inspire Charles Darwin. On the mainland, Amazon lodges offer river trips and rainforest walks, and the coast has beaches and seasonal whale watching.

The highlands are generally driest from June to September. The coast is warmer and drier from December to May, while the Galápagos can be visited year-round, with warmer, calmer seas in the first half of the year and cooler, wildlife-rich waters later on.

Ecuador: Money

Ecuador's currency is the US dollar (USD). The country has used the dollar since 2000, and locally minted coins circulate alongside US coins.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are accepted in hotels, larger restaurants and shops in cities, but cash is essential for markets, buses, taxis and smaller towns. Carry small bills; $50 and $100 notes can be hard to break. In the Galápagos, plan to have cash for park fees and many small purchases.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are available in Quito, Guayaquil, Cuenca and other larger towns, but they can be scarce in rural areas and on some islands. Withdraw ahead of remote travel, and watch for local and home-bank fees.
  • Tipping: Many restaurants add a 10% service charge to the bill. An extra tip is optional and appreciated for good service. Tour guides are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: If you arrive with dollars in cash, bring clean, smaller notes. Other currencies can be exchanged at banks or licensed offices in cities, but you will spend dollars day to day.
  • Common scams: Count your change, especially when paying with large notes, and check the amount on card machines before you pay. Be cautious at ATMs and of anyone offering unsolicited help.

Paraguay

A bilingual heartland of Jesuit missions, rivers and unhurried hospitality.

Paraguay is one of South America's least-visited countries, and that is part of its appeal. Spanish and Guaraní are both official languages, and many people speak both in daily life. The culture is warm and unhurried, with tereré, a cold yerba mate drink, shared among friends, and asado and chipá, a cheesy cassava bread, on many tables.

Asunción, the riverside capital, mixes historic buildings, lively markets and a growing food scene. Near the tri-border with Brazil and Argentina, Ciudad del Este is a busy commercial city, and the nearby Saltos del Monday are a powerful waterfall set in the forest. The Jesuit Missions of La Santísima Trinidad de Paraná and Jesús de Tavarangue, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites, are among the country's most moving historic places. To the west, the Gran Chaco opens into a vast, sparsely populated wilderness.

The milder months, roughly April to September, are generally the most comfortable time to visit, with less intense heat than in the southern summer.

Paraguay: Money

Paraguay's currency is the guaraní (PYG), often written with a ₲ sign. Prices run into the thousands and tens of thousands, so take a moment to count the zeros before you pay.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely used in malls, hotels, supermarkets and many restaurants in Asunción. Buses, taxis, markets, small cafés and towns outside the capital are often cash only. Some hotels and border businesses may quote prices in US dollars; ask for the total in guaraníes.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are easiest to find in Asunción, Ciudad del Este and other cities, usually inside banks or shopping centers. Local and home-bank fees are common, so fewer, larger withdrawals can help. Choose to be charged in guaraníes if the machine offers a conversion to your home currency.
  • Tipping: Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory. Around 5 to 10% is a kind gesture in restaurants when a service charge is not already on the bill. Rounding up is enough in casual spots.
  • Exchanging money: Licensed exchange offices in city centers usually offer better rates than airports or hotels. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest to exchange. Count your guaraníes at the counter and ask for a receipt.
  • Common scams: Watch for counterfeit notes, confusing exchange calculations and undeclared card surcharges. Confirm the final price before you pay, especially in busy border areas such as Ciudad del Este.

Brazil is the largest country in South America, and its size brings incredible diversity. From the Amazon rainforest in the north to the beaches and Atlantic Forest of the south, it's a country of natural beauty, warm people and a culture full of music, dance and celebration. Portuguese is the official language, and Brazilians are famous for their hospitality and joyful spirit.

Our Brazil section focuses on two very different coastal favorites. Rio de Janeiro is the iconic "Marvelous City," with Sugarloaf Mountain, Christ the Redeemer and the legendary beaches of Copacabana and Ipanema. Further south in Santa Catarina, Balneário Camboriú pairs a striking skyline with golden sand, cable car rides and quiet coves nearby. Both offer great food, lively nightlife and plenty of sunshine, and we offer our own tours in each.

Brazil: Money

Brazil's currency is the Brazilian real (BRL), written with the symbol R$. Paying is easy in most places, and cards and digital payments are part of everyday life.

  • Cash vs. card: Credit and debit cards are accepted almost everywhere, from restaurants to supermarkets. Brazilians also use Pix, an instant payment system, but it usually needs a Brazilian bank account. Carry a little cash for beach vendors, street markets and small kiosks.
  • ATMs and fees: Use ATMs inside banks, shopping malls or airports instead of standalone machines on the street. Some ATMs limit withdrawals at night, and both your home bank and the local machine may charge a fee, so take out larger amounts less often.
  • Tipping: Restaurants usually add a 10% service charge ("serviço") to the bill. It's customary but optional. Beyond that, tipping isn't expected, though rounding up for good service is always appreciated.
  • Exchanging money: Licensed exchange offices (casas de câmbio) in city centers and malls usually offer better rates than airport counters. Many travelers find withdrawing reais from an ATM simpler.
  • Common scams: Check the amount on the card machine before you tap or enter your PIN, keep your card in sight, and count your change, especially in busy tourist areas.

Guyana

Rainforest, mighty waterfalls and South America's only English-speaking country.

Guyana is South America's only English-speaking country, a place of vast rainforest, wide rivers and a rich blend of Indigenous, Afro-Guyanese, Indo-Guyanese and other cultures. Most people live along the Atlantic coast, in and around the capital, Georgetown, a city of wooden colonial buildings, lively markets and a historic botanic garden.

The country's greatest natural treasure is Kaieteur Falls, a single, towering drop deep in the rainforest, usually reached by a small plane from Georgetown. Further inland, the Iwokrama Forest and the Rupununi savannah offer wildlife, community-run lodges and a sense of remote wilderness far from the usual tourist trail.

The drier periods, roughly February to April and August to November, are generally the most practical times to travel, with better conditions for flights to Kaieteur and for exploring the interior. Rain can still fall in any month, so a flexible itinerary is wise.

Guyana: Money

Guyana's currency is the Guyanese dollar (GYD), often written with a $ sign. US dollars are also accepted in some hotels, tour offices and shops, especially in Georgetown.

  • Cash vs. card: Cash is essential outside the capital and useful even in town. Cards are accepted at some hotels, restaurants and supermarkets in Georgetown, but you should not rely on them in the interior or at smaller businesses.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are concentrated in Georgetown and a few larger towns, and not all accept foreign cards. Withdraw what you need before traveling inland or flying to Kaieteur Falls. Fees may apply.
  • Tipping: Around 10% is appreciated in restaurants when a service charge is not already included. Rounding up for taxis is common, and guides on jungle or falls trips are often tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Licensed cambios and banks in Georgetown are the safest places to exchange money. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to change. Avoid informal street offers.
  • Common scams: Count your change carefully, agree on taxi fares in advance, and be wary of unofficial money changers.

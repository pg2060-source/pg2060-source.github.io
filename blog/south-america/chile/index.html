Chile

A long, narrow land of desert, vineyards, volcanoes and Patagonian wilderness.

Chile stretches like a ribbon between the Andes and the Pacific, packing an astonishing range of landscapes into one long, narrow country. Spanish is the official language. In the north lies the Atacama Desert, one of the driest places on Earth, with salt flats, geysers and some of the clearest night skies in the world. Further south, the Lake District is a region of volcanoes, forests and lakeside towns, while Patagonia offers glaciers, fjords and the granite towers of Torres del Paine. Off the mainland, Easter Island (Rapa Nui) is famous for its monumental moai statues.

Santiago is a lively capital with a growing food scene and easy access to nearby wine valleys and, in winter, ski resorts. Colorful Valparaíso, a hillside port of steep streets and murals, makes a memorable stop along the coast.

Because Chile covers so many climates, the best time to visit depends on where you go. Southern summer, roughly November to March, is ideal for Patagonia. The Atacama is rewarding year-round, and Santiago is especially pleasant in spring and autumn.

Chile: Money

Chile's currency is the Chilean peso (CLP), written with a $ sign, so double-check whether a price is in pesos or US dollars.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely accepted, even in many smaller towns. Still carry some pesos for remote areas, small stalls and places where card machines go offline. Foreign tourists without residence in Chile may be exempt from VAT on hotel accommodation at registered properties when payment is received in foreign currency, including, at many hotels, a foreign-issued card. Ask when you book, as not every property participates.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are easy to find in cities and towns. Local machines often charge a withdrawal fee, and your home bank may add one too, so fewer, larger withdrawals usually work better. Choose to be charged in pesos if the machine offers a conversion to your home currency.
  • Tipping: Around 10% is customary in restaurants. A suggested tip ("propina") is often added to the bill or offered as an option, so check before adding more.
  • Exchanging money: Banks and licensed exchange offices generally offer better rates than airports or hotels. Many travelers find withdrawing pesos from an ATM simpler.
  • Common scams: Cover the keypad at ATMs, keep your card in sight, and be cautious of distraction tricks in busy areas. Count your change in markets and on local buses.

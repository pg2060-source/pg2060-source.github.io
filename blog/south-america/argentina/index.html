Argentina

Wild nature, rich traditions and some of the world's most stunning waterfalls.

Argentina stretches from the subtropical north all the way down to Patagonia, offering an astonishing range of landscapes along the way: the Andes, vineyards, glaciers, grasslands and rainforest. It's a country of passionate traditions, from tango and mate to long, sociable dinners and famous Argentine beef. Spanish is the official language.

Our Argentina section begins in the northeast, in the province of Misiones, home to Puerto Iguazú and the breathtaking Iguazú Falls. Surrounded by lush rainforest and protected as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the falls are one of the most impressive natural sights in South America, and the Argentine side lets you walk right up to the edge of the water. It's a must for nature lovers, and we offer our own tours there.

Argentina: Money

Argentina's currency is the Argentine peso (ARS), also written with a $ sign, so double-check whether a price is in pesos or US dollars. Prices can change quickly, so it's worth checking the latest rate before and during your trip.

  • Official vs. "blue" dollar: Argentina has more than one exchange rate. The official rate is the one set through banks, and the "blue" dollar is the informal street rate. For years the blue rate was far higher than the official one, but the gap has narrowed a lot recently. Our currency converter shows the official rate, so treat it as a guide and check current rates before you exchange. Street money changers (often called "arbolitos") are informal and carry a risk of counterfeit notes.
  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely accepted in cities and tourist areas, and foreign cards often get a rate close to the market rate. Smaller shops, taxis and markets may prefer cash, and some places offer a discount for paying in cash.
  • ATMs and fees: ATM withdrawals for foreign cards can carry high fees and low limits per transaction, so plan ahead and compare options. Money transfer services that let you pick up pesos in cash are another option many visitors use.
  • Tipping: Around 10% is customary in restaurants. It's usually not included in the bill, and it's often appreciated in cash.
  • Common scams: Learn what genuine notes look like and check bills you receive, especially larger ones. Count your change carefully in busy places.

Puerto Iguazú

Iguazú Falls · Argentina

  1. Blog
  2. South America
  3. Argentina
  4. Puerto Iguazú

Puerto Iguazú, in Argentina's Misiones province, is the gateway to one of the natural wonders of the world. Iguazú Falls stretches along the border with Brazil as a chain of roughly 275 individual waterfalls surrounded by lush subtropical rainforest, protected in Iguazú National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

On the Argentine side, walkways bring you remarkably close to the water. Take the eco-train to the Devil's Throat (Garganta del Diablo), where the river plunges into a roaring horseshoe of mist, then explore the Upper and Lower Circuits for different angles. Boat rides beneath the falls are thrilling (expect to get soaked), and keep an eye out for toucans, butterflies and coatis. Back in town, visit the Three Borders Landmark, where Argentina, Brazil and Paraguay meet.

The falls are impressive year-round. March to May and August to November offer the most pleasant temperatures, while summer is hot and humid but often brings a powerful water flow. Check our tours in Iguazú.

Travel tips

  • Getting there: Cataratas del Iguazú International Airport (IGR) is about 20 km from town. You can also fly into Foz do Iguaçu (IGU) on the Brazilian side and cross the border.
  • Getting around: Regular buses run from the town's bus terminal to Iguazú National Park in about 30 minutes. Taxis and tours are also available.
  • Food to try: Local river fish such as surubí, chipá (cheese bread) and, of course, a classic Argentine asado.
  • Practical tip: Plan a full day for the Argentine side, bring a rain poncho or a change of clothes for the boat rides, and carry your passport if you want to visit the Brazilian side.
  • Wildlife tip: Never feed the coatis and keep your food out of sight. They are bold, can bite and will grab snacks.

Antarctica is unlike anywhere else on Earth: a frozen continent of towering glaciers, ice-covered mountains and floating icebergs in every shade of blue. It has no permanent population, apart from scientists who live at research stations, and it is protected by the Antarctic Treaty, which reserves the continent for peace and science.

Most travelers reach Antarctica on expedition cruises, many of them departing from Ushuaia in southern Argentina and crossing the famous Drake Passage. Once there, the rewards are extraordinary: colonies of penguins, seals resting on the ice, whales surfacing beside the ship and Zodiac landings on remote shores where few people have ever set foot.

Trips run only during the southern summer, roughly November to March, when temperatures are milder and the long daylight hours make the landscape glow. It's a once-in-a-lifetime journey for nature lovers.

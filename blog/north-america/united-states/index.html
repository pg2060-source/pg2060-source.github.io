The United States is a vast country of national parks, restless cities and coastlines that run from the subtropics to the Arctic. English is the primary language, and Spanish is widely spoken in many regions. The variety is the point: desert canyons and Pacific surf, the skylines of New York and Chicago, the music of New Orleans and Nashville, the national parks of the West, and the distinct cultures of places such as Alaska, Hawaii and the Southwest.

Travelers come for road trips and city breaks, for world-class museums and for landscapes protected in a large network of national parks and seashores. Food, too, is regional, from New England to the Pacific Northwest, and is often as memorable as the sights.

The best time to visit depends entirely on where you go. Late spring through early autumn suits most national parks and northern cities, while the southern and western deserts are often more comfortable in winter and early spring. Hurricane season can affect the Atlantic and Gulf coasts in summer and autumn, and mountain roads may close in winter.

United States: Money

The currency of the United States is the US dollar (USD), written with a $ sign. Cards are part of everyday life, and cash is less essential than in many other countries, though it is still useful in some places.

  • Cash vs. card: Credit and debit cards, including contactless payments, are accepted almost everywhere. A small number of restaurants, market stalls and rural businesses are cash only, or they may set a minimum charge for cards, so it is wise to carry some dollars in small bills. Sales tax is usually added at the register rather than included in the listed price, and the rate varies by state and city.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are widely available in cities, towns and many national park areas, usually inside banks, stores or hotel lobbies. Out-of-network and foreign-card fees are common, so fewer, larger withdrawals can help. Choose to be charged in US dollars if the machine offers a conversion to your home currency.
  • Tipping: Tipping is customary in restaurants, bars, taxis, ride-shares and many personal-service settings, and it is an important part of how service workers are paid. In sit-down restaurants, around 15 to 20% is a typical range before tax. A service charge or automatic gratuity is sometimes added for larger groups, so check the bill before adding more.
  • Exchanging money: Currency-exchange desks are found at major airports and in some banks in large cities, but rates are not always favorable. Visitors arriving from abroad often withdraw dollars from an ATM or simply pay by card for most purchases.
  • Common scams: Check the amount on the card terminal before you pay, be cautious of ATM skimming in tourist areas, and count your change if you pay in cash.

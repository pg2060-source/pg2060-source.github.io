Trinidad and Tobago is a two-island nation just off the coast of South America, with a character shaped by African, Indian, European and Caribbean influences. English is the official language. Trinidad is the larger, more urban island and the home of Carnival, steelpan and calypso, with Port of Spain as a lively capital and rainforested hills and wetlands close at hand. In season, leatherback turtles nest on the north coast, and the island is a favorite with birdwatchers.

Tobago, a short flight or ferry ride away, is slower and more beach-oriented, with coral reefs, protected marine areas and a rainforest reserve in the interior. Many travelers split time between Tobago's coast and a few days in Trinidad for culture and nature.

The drier months, roughly January to May, are generally the most comfortable. Carnival usually takes place in February or early March and should be planned well ahead. June to November is the wetter season, though Tobago's reefs and rainforests are rewarding year-round.

Trinidad and Tobago: Money

Trinidad and Tobago's currency is the Trinidad and Tobago dollar (TTD), often written as TT$. US dollars are sometimes accepted at hotels and in tourist businesses, especially in Tobago, but everyday prices are in local dollars.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely used in Port of Spain, at larger hotels and in many restaurants and shops. Taxis, maxi-taxis, smaller eateries, markets and some beach bars in Tobago are often cash only. During Carnival, cash is especially useful. Confirm whether a quoted price is in Trinidad and Tobago or US dollars.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are easy to find in Port of Spain, at the main airports and in Tobago's larger towns, and they generally dispense local dollars. They can be scarcer in rural parts of both islands, so withdraw ahead of those trips. Fees may apply; choose to be charged in Trinidad and Tobago dollars if a conversion is offered.
  • Tipping: Around 10% is appreciated in restaurants when a service charge is not already included. Check the bill, as a service charge is sometimes added. Tour guides, turtle-watching guides and taxi drivers are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Banks and licensed exchange bureaus in the cities and at the airports are the safest places to change money. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to exchange. Avoid informal offers.
  • Common scams: Count your change, especially in crowded Carnival settings, and keep your card in sight when you pay.

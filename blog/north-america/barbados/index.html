Barbados

Coral shores, rum heritage and a UNESCO-listed historic port city.

Barbados is a coral island in the eastern Caribbean with a character of its own, a proud rum and cricket culture, and a coastline that changes from west to east. English is the official language. The west, or "Platinum," coast is known for calm Caribbean water and polished beach hotels, while the east coast faces the Atlantic with dramatic cliffs and rougher surf. Historic Bridgetown and its Garrison, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, make a rewarding introduction to the island's history.

Travelers come for swimming and snorkeling, for a taste of Bajan cuisine, and for visits to rum distilleries, botanical gardens and underground caves such as Harrison's Cave. The island is compact and easy to explore by car or organized tour.

December to April is the driest and busiest season. The rest of the year is warmer and rainier, with the Crop Over festival in midsummer offering a lively window on local music and culture.

Barbados: Money

Barbados's currency is the Barbados dollar (BBD), often written as Bds$. It is pegged to the US dollar, and US dollars are widely accepted, especially in tourist areas.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are accepted at hotels, restaurants and many shops, particularly on the south and west coasts. Carry some Barbados dollars for taxis, rum shops, markets and smaller beach vendors. Confirm whether a price is in Barbados or US dollars before you pay; change is often given in local currency.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are widely available in Bridgetown, at the airport and in tourist areas, and they generally dispense Barbados dollars. Local and home-bank fees are common, so fewer, larger withdrawals can help. Choose to be charged in Barbados dollars if the machine offers a conversion to your home currency.
  • Tipping: A service charge of around 10% is often added in hotels and restaurants. If it is already included, an extra tip is optional. If not, around 10 to 15% is appreciated for good service. Tour guides are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Banks and licensed exchange offices generally offer clearer rates than hotel desks. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to change.

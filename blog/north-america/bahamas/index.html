The Bahamas is an island chain of clear, shallow water, white-sand cays and a rhythm that shifts from island to island. English is the official language. Nassau, on New Providence, is the lively capital, with a historic center, a busy harbor and the resorts of nearby Paradise Island. Just beyond the capital, the water is the main attraction: warm, transparent and ideal for swimming, snorkeling and boating.

Many travelers continue to the Exumas for sandbars and quiet cays, to Eleuthera and Harbour Island for pink-tinged beaches, or to the more laid-back Family Islands, where life moves more slowly than in Nassau. Diving, fishing and simply lingering on the beach are all part of the appeal.

December to April is generally the driest and most popular period. The summer and autumn months are warmer and more humid, and they coincide with the Atlantic hurricane season.

Bahamas: Money

The Bahamas' currency is the Bahamian dollar (BSD), often written as B$. It is pegged to the US dollar, and US dollars are widely accepted throughout the islands.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely used in Nassau, Paradise Island and larger hotels and restaurants. On the smaller Family Islands, cash is much more important, and some businesses may be cash only. Prices may be quoted in Bahamian or US dollars; the two are often used interchangeably, and change may be given in either currency.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are easy to find in Nassau and other major islands, but they can be scarce or unreliable in more remote settlements. Withdraw what you need before heading to a smaller island. Fees may apply, so fewer, larger withdrawals are often more economical.
  • Tipping: Many hotels and restaurants add a gratuity or service charge, often around 15%. Check the bill before adding more. If a tip is not included, around 15% is customary in restaurants. Tour guides, boat crews and taxi drivers are often tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Because US dollars circulate so widely, many visitors simply arrive with dollars in mixed small bills and use them alongside Bahamian dollars. Banks and licensed exchange desks are available in Nassau if you need to change another currency.
  • Common scams: Confirm the currency of a quoted price, count your change, and keep your card in sight when you pay.

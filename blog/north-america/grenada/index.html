Grenada

Spice-scented hills, a horseshoe harbor and some of the Caribbean's loveliest beaches.

Grenada, often called the Spice Isle, is a lush Caribbean nation of nutmeg and cinnamon trees, a deep-water harbor and some of the region's most beautiful beaches. English is the official language. St. George's, the capital, rises in terracotta and pastel around a horseshoe harbor, with markets, forts and hillside views. Grand Anse is the island's best-known stretch of sand, and just offshore, an underwater sculpture park has become a favorite for snorkelers and divers.

Inland, hiking trails lead through rainforest around Grand Etang, and spice estates and rum distilleries offer a taste of what still grows on the hillsides. The smaller islands of Carriacou and Petite Martinique are a short ferry ride away and feel even more unhurried.

December to April is the driest and most popular season. The rest of the year is hotter and rainier, and June to November is the hurricane season.

Grenada: Money

Grenada's currency is the East Caribbean dollar (XCD), often written as EC$. It is pegged to the US dollar, and US dollars are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants and tourist shops.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are accepted at many hotels, restaurants and larger shops in St. George's and around Grand Anse. Carry cash for local buses, smaller eateries, markets and trips to Carriacou or Petite Martinique. Confirm whether a quoted price is in East Caribbean or US dollars; change is often given in local currency.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are available in St. George's and other parts of the main island, and they generally dispense East Caribbean dollars. They can be limited on the smaller islands, so withdraw before you go. Fees may apply.
  • Tipping: A service charge of around 10% is often added in hotels and restaurants. If it is already included, an extra tip is optional. If not, around 10 to 15% is appreciated. Boat and tour guides are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Banks in St. George's and hotel desks can change cash. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to use or exchange. Choose to be charged in East Caribbean dollars if a machine offers a conversion to your home currency.

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a nation of one main island and a long chain of smaller Grenadine islands, beloved by sailors for their cays, reefs and quiet anchorages. English is the official language. Saint Vincent itself is green and volcanic, with waterfalls, a rugged windward coast and the chance to hike La Soufrière when conditions allow. Kingstown, the capital, is a working harbor town with a botanic garden that is among the oldest in the Western Hemisphere.

The Grenadines are the main draw for many visitors: Bequia with its relaxed waterfront, the Tobago Cays Marine Park for snorkeling over clear, protected reefs, and a scatter of other islands, some quiet, some exclusive. The best way to see them is often by ferry, schooner or a short-hop flight.

December to April is the driest and most popular sailing season. The rest of the year is wetter and warmer, and June to November is the hurricane season.

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines: Money

The currency of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is the East Caribbean dollar (XCD), often written as EC$. It is pegged to the US dollar, and US dollars are widely accepted in hotels, yacht facilities and tourist businesses.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are accepted at some hotels, restaurants and shops in Kingstown and on more developed islands such as Bequia, but cash is essential once you move through the smaller Grenadines. There may be no working card machine on a cay or a quiet island, so bring enough cash for boats, park fees, meals and lodging. Confirm whether a price is in East Caribbean or US dollars.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are concentrated in Kingstown and a few larger settlements, and they generally dispense East Caribbean dollars. Do not count on finding a machine in the Tobago Cays or on smaller islands. Withdraw what you need before you sail or fly down the chain, and expect fees.
  • Tipping: A service charge is sometimes added in hotels and restaurants. If it is not, around 10 to 15% is appreciated. Boat crews, skippers and water-taxi operators are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Banks in Kingstown are the most practical place to change money. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to use or exchange, and they are useful to have in small bills on the islands.

Saint Kitts and Nevis

Fortress views, rainforest slopes and two relaxed sister islands.

Saint Kitts and Nevis is a two-island nation in the eastern Caribbean, with rainforest-covered volcanoes, fortress views and a relaxed, unhurried feel. English is the official language. On Saint Kitts, Basseterre is a compact capital, and the island's centerpiece is Brimstone Hill Fortress, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with sweeping views of the sea. Beaches, a scenic railway along the coast and hiking on Mount Liamuiga give a mix of easy days and more active ones.

Sister island Nevis is smaller and quieter, with a single volcanic cone, historic inns set in old estates, and beaches that feel a long way from the larger Caribbean resorts. A short ferry links the two, so many travelers split their time.

December to April is the driest and most popular season. The rest of the year is warmer and more humid, and June to November is the hurricane season.

Saint Kitts and Nevis: Money

Saint Kitts and Nevis's currency is the East Caribbean dollar (XCD), often written as EC$. It is pegged to the US dollar, and US dollars are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants and tourist shops.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are accepted at many hotels, restaurants and shops in Basseterre and the main tourist areas, and on Nevis at larger inns and restaurants. Carry cash for taxis, smaller eateries and some beach bars, and confirm whether a price is in East Caribbean or US dollars. Change is often given in local currency.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are available in Basseterre and in a few other locations, including on Nevis, and they generally dispense East Caribbean dollars. They are not on every corner, so withdraw what you need rather than counting on the next machine. Fees may apply.
  • Tipping: A service charge of around 10% is often added in hotels and restaurants. If it is already included, an extra tip is optional. If not, around 10 to 15% is appreciated. Taxi drivers and guides are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Banks and hotel desks can change cash. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to use or exchange. Choose to be charged in East Caribbean dollars if a machine offers a conversion to your home currency.

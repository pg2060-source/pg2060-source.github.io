Dominica

A lush "Nature Island" of rainforests, waterfalls and a boiling volcanic lake.

Dominica, often called the Nature Island of the Caribbean, is a rugged, volcanic country of rainforests, rivers and waterfalls rather than long, flat beaches. English is the official language. Morne Trois Pitons National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is the centerpiece, with trails that lead to the Boiling Lake, steam vents and waterfalls such as Trafalgar and Middleham. The coast offers black-sand coves and snorkeling at Champagne Reef, where bubbles rise from underwater vents.

In the northeast, the Kalinago Territory offers an introduction to the island's Indigenous culture. Roseau, the compact capital, and the mountain village of Laudat are common starting points for days in the forest.

January to April is generally the driest and most popular hiking season. The rest of the year is greener and wetter, and June to November is the hurricane season, when trails can be slippery and some river crossings more difficult.

Dominica: Money

Dominica's currency is the East Caribbean dollar (XCD), often written as EC$. It is pegged to the US dollar, and US dollars are accepted at many hotels and tour desks.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are accepted at some hotels, larger restaurants and a few shops in Roseau, but cash is essential for trail fees, smaller guesthouses, local buses, snacks and many nature sites. Confirm whether a price is in East Caribbean or US dollars. Carry enough cash into the interior, where card machines may be scarce or offline.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are concentrated in Roseau and a few other towns, and they generally dispense East Caribbean dollars. They are not something to count on once you leave the capital. Withdraw what you need before a multi-day hiking trip, and expect fees.
  • Tipping: A service charge is sometimes added in hotels and restaurants. If it is not, around 10% is a kind gesture for good service. Hiking and boat guides are typically tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Banks in Roseau are the most practical place to change money. Clean US dollar notes are the easiest foreign currency to use or exchange. Choose to be charged in East Caribbean dollars if a machine offers a conversion to your home currency.

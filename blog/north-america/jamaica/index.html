Jamaica is a mountainous Caribbean island with a cultural influence far larger than its size, from reggae and dancehall to jerk cooking and a worldwide musical and sporting heritage. English is the official language, and Jamaican Patois is widely spoken. The north and west coasts are lined with well-known beach towns, including Montego Bay, Ocho Rios and Negril, each with its own pace, from lively resorts to long, sunset-facing beaches.

Inland, the Blue Mountains rise above Kingston, the creative, restless capital, and are known for coffee and cool hiking trails. Dunn's River Falls, river rafting and time in smaller communities beyond the resort strips give a fuller picture of the island.

December to April is the driest and most popular season. Summer is hotter and more humid, and the official hurricane season runs from June to November, though the mountains mean the weather can vary from coast to interior on the same day.

Jamaica: Money

Jamaica's currency is the Jamaican dollar (JMD), often written as J$. US dollars are widely accepted in resort areas, but prices are in Jamaican dollars, and paying in the local currency is usually clearer.

  • Cash vs. card: Cards are widely used in hotels, resorts and many restaurants in Montego Bay, Ocho Rios, Negril and Kingston. Taxis, jerk stands, markets and smaller towns are often cash only. If you pay in US dollars, change may come back in Jamaican dollars, sometimes at a less favorable rate.
  • ATMs and fees: ATMs are easy to find in cities and tourist areas, usually at banks or shopping plazas, and they generally dispense Jamaican dollars. They can be scarce in the mountains and in smaller rural communities. Fees are common; choose to be charged in Jamaican dollars if a conversion is offered.
  • Tipping: A service charge is sometimes added in hotels and restaurants, so check the bill. If it is not included, around 10 to 15% is customary in restaurants. Tour guides, drivers and hotel staff are often tipped in cash.
  • Exchanging money: Use banks, cambios or official hotel desks rather than informal street offers. Bring clean US dollar notes if you plan to exchange cash.
  • Common scams: Confirm whether a price is in Jamaican or US dollars, count your change, and be cautious of unofficial money changers and of distraction tricks at ATMs.
